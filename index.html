FIELD METHODS & LIMITATIONS

Research and measurement guide

How to use NoiseLab responsibly for environmental acoustic investigations.

01 · BEFORE RECORDING

Preparation

Prefer an external measurement-grade microphone with documented frequency response. Verify calibration with a suitable acoustic calibrator if compatible; use a reference sound level meter for independent cross-checks. Avoid wind, mechanical contact, obstructions and reflections. Set microphone height/location and the sampling duration in your study protocol.

Chrome/Edge may request microphone permission. The app asks to disable automatic gain control, noise suppression and echo cancellation, but the device/browser might override these settings. An active audio path and unchanged gain are essential.

02 · REFERENCE COMPARISON

Calibrate and validate

Capture steady sound for 10 seconds while a reference meter records the same interval. Afterward, enter the reference meter's actual A-weighted equivalent level and add the paired observation. Repeat at multiple sound levels; save the median offset only after inspecting the variation. Use independent validation measurements that were not used to estimate the offset.

A one-frequency sensitivity calibration does not demonstrate broadband frequency-response compliance. Any change of microphone, device, browser audio path, or gain configuration requires reassessment.

03 · MEASUREMENT DEFINITIONS

What is being calculated?

LAeq,T is 10 × log₁₀ of the mean squared A-weighted signal after calibration. FAST (125 ms) and SLOW (1 s) use exponential time averaging. LAFmax/min are extrema of sampled 100 ms FAST readings during a survey; they can miss inter-frame extrema. L10/L50/L90 use percentiles of these sampled FAST readings.

A/C weighting filters are bilinear-transformed digital approximations normalized at 1 kHz, not independently certified IEC frequency-weighting responses. Do not use this application for legal or occupational noise compliance decisions.

05 · DATA PRIVACY

Local data and exports

Raw audio remains in the AudioWorklet processing stream and is never saved or uploaded. Calibration profiles are stored in localStorage; completed surveys in IndexedDB. CSV exports contain per-second energy levels and metadata; JSON backup contains all saved surveys. Deleting browser site data can remove your records.

Location is requested only when you click Get GPS location. Geolocation requires permission and a secure origin (GitHub Pages supports HTTPS).
